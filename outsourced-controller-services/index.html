Signs It's Time

Is It Time for a Controller?

Bookkeeping keeps transactions current. A controller makes sure the numbers are right, close on time, and defensible.

  • ✓ Your bookkeeper is capable, but no one is reviewing their work or owning the close.
  • ✓ Month-end close regularly slips past the 15th, or you’re not sure when it’s actually done.
  • ✓ You’ve had surprises in your financials that should have been caught earlier.
  • ✓ You’re preparing for an audit, due diligence, or a bank covenant review and need real internal controls.
  • ✓ Revenue recognition, accruals, or multi-entity consolidation have gotten more complex than your current process can handle.

Common Questions

Outsourced Controller Services — Frequently Asked Questions

What’s the difference between a controller and a bookkeeper?
−
A bookkeeper records transactions. A controller owns the accuracy of the close — reviewing entries, enforcing internal controls, and making sure your financial statements are correct and defensible, often overseeing bookkeeping staff.
What’s the difference between an outsourced controller and a fractional CFO?
+
A controller is focused on the accuracy of the past — closing the books correctly. A CFO is focused on the future — strategy, forecasting, and capital decisions. Many clients start with controller-level support and add CFO-level strategy as they grow.
Can you work alongside our existing bookkeeper or accounting staff?
+
Yes — this is one of the most common engagement types. We provide the review, oversight, and process discipline on top of the team you already have.
How quickly can you close our books each month?
+
Timeline depends on transaction volume and current process maturity, but a well-run close should land within 10-15 business days of month-end. We’ll build a close calendar and hold to it.
Do you work with businesses outside of Florida?
+
Yes. We work with clients across the United States. Most controller-level engagements are managed remotely with regular video check-ins.
